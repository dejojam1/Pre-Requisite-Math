    Section 1.1 Fractions and Percentages

    Subsection 1.1.1 Converting Fractions

    Fractions greater than one come in two forms:
    🔗
    • Improper Fractions have a greater numerator than denominator, for example \(\frac{7}{6}\text{.}\)
      🔗
    • Mixed Fractions identify the greatest integer that is less than the fraction, and the remainder, for example \(1\frac{1}{6}\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗
    Improper fractions are commonly preferred for calculations, but to interpret a fraction, writing it in mixed form is useful. We need to be able to convert between the forms.
    🔗

    Example 1.1.1. Converting to a Mixed Fraction.

    Write \(\frac{37}{7}\) as a mixed fraction.
    🔗
    Solution.
    We first ask ’How many times does 7 go into 37?’ Now \(5\times 7=35\) and \(6\times 7=42\) which is too big, so the answer is ’5 times’. This is the integer in our mixed fraction.
    🔗
    The remainder is \(37-35=2\text{,}\) and so
    \begin{equation*} \frac{37}{7}=5\frac{2}{7}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.1.3. Converting to an Improper Fraction.

    Write \(3\frac{6}{11}\) as an improper fraction.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we have 3 as our integer. Since we are working with elevenths, \(1=\frac{11}{11}\text{.}\) Then \(3=3\left(\frac{11}{11}\right)=\frac{33}{11}\text{.}\) We have an additional \(\frac{6}{11}\text{,}\) and so
    \begin{equation*} 3\frac{6}{11}=\frac{33+6}{11}=\frac{39}{11}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.1.2 Reducing Fractions

    Where possible we want to simplify fractions. Fractions where the numerator and denominator have no common factors (other than 1 and -1) are said to be in reduced or lowest form . For example, \(\frac{5}{6}\) is in reduced form, since 5 and 6 have no common factors. On the other hand, \(\frac{4}{6}\) is not in reduced form, since 4 and 6 are both divisible by 2 (they have a common factor of 2).
    🔗

    Example 1.1.5. Reducing Fractions.

    Write \(\frac{30}{35}\) in reduced form.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Both 30 and 35 are divisible by 5. In particular \(30=6\times 5\) and \(35=7\times 5\text{.}\) We can cancel out the common factor of 5 to simplify the fraction.
    \begin{equation*} \frac{30}{35}=\frac{6\times 5}{7\times 5}=\frac{6}{7}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
