    Section 2.1 Irrational Numbers

    Irrational numbers are ones that cannot be written as fractions. We will not go into much detail until later in the course, but there are two common types of irrational numbers that you should be aware of:
    🔗
    • \(\pi=3.14159265...\text{,}\) and
      🔗
    • square roots.
      🔗
    🔗
    You are probably quite familiar with some square roots, such as \(\sqrt{1}=1\text{,}\) \(\sqrt{4}=2\text{,}\) and \(\sqrt{9}=3\text{,}\) as 1, 4, and 9 are perfect squares. Square roots of other numbers can be more challenging to work with.
    🔗

    Example 2.1.1. Square Root of 3.

    How could we determine where to place \(\sqrt{3}\) on a number line?
    🔗
    Solution.
    If we had a calculator, we could calculate \(\sqrt{3}=1.732...\text{.}\) Without a calculator we cannot expect to be this precise, but we should be able to get an idea of how large this number is by comparing with other values.
    🔗
    We know that \(\sqrt{1}=1\) and \(\sqrt{4}=2\text{.}\) Now
    \begin{equation*} 1\lt 3\lt 4. \end{equation*}
    The square root function always increases; a greater number has a greater square root, and a lower number has a lower square root. This means that
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{1}\lt \sqrt{3}\lt \sqrt{4}, \end{equation*}
    or in other words
    \begin{equation*} 1\lt \sqrt{3}\lt 2. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Note also that
    \begin{equation*} 1\lt \sqrt{2}\lt \sqrt{3}, \end{equation*}
    so we might not expect \(\sqrt{3}\) to be close to the left-hand side of the interval between 1 and 2. Therefore we might guess that \(\sqrt{3}\) should go somewhere between 1.5 and 2. This isn’t very precise, but it gives us a general idea of the size of \(\sqrt{3}\) for comparison with other numbers.
    🔗
    If you wanted to narrow down the placement, you could calculate
    \begin{equation*} (1.5)^2=2.25. \end{equation*}
    This confirms that \(\sqrt{3}\) should be to the right of 1.5 on the number line. We could then calculate
    \begin{equation*} (1.75)^2=3.0625. \end{equation*}
    Then \(\sqrt{3}\) should be slightly to the left of 1.75 on the number line. This process could be continued to the level of accuracy desired.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
